WHAT THIS
ACTUALLY IS

If you've never heard of Toastmasters before, here's the short version, no jargon.

In One Line
A club where students practice speaking and leading, in short structured sessions, by actually doing it — not by studying theory.
The Basics, No Fluff

Three things people usually get wrong.

Myth
"It's an English-speaking class."
Reality
It's leadership + communication practice. Grammar isn't the point — thinking on your feet and running a room is.
Myth
"You have to be good at public speaking to join."
Reality
Most people join because they're not good at it yet. That's the entire point of showing up.
Myth
"It's just a college club with no real structure."
Reality
There's an actual credentialed program behind it (Pathways) with real levels and certificates — more on that below.
Roles You'll Rotate Through

Every meeting has jobs. You'll try all of them eventually.

Speaker — gives a prepared talk on a topic of their choice.
Evaluator — gives structured, specific feedback to a speaker.
Table Topics Master — runs the impromptu speaking segment.
Toastmaster of the Day — hosts and runs the entire meeting.
Timer / Grammarian / Ah-Counter — tracks specific details during the meeting.

We're affiliated with Toastmasters International

Toastmasters International is the global nonprofit organization behind this format — it's been running structured public speaking and leadership clubs worldwide since 1924, across thousands of clubs in over 140 countries. Our club follows its meeting format and uses its official education program (Pathways).

Why This, Not Another Club

Most clubs are one activity. This is a transferable skill.

A Typical Club

  • Builds one specific skill
  • Useful mostly inside that club
  • Practice stays inside the room

Toastmasters

  • Builds communication + leadership
  • Useful in interviews, class, every job
  • Structured path with real certifications (Pathways)
